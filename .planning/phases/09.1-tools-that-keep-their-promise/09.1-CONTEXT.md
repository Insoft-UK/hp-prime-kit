# Phase 9.1 context: Tools that keep their promise

## What this phase is

Two tools, two promises. `hpprime run` promises that what it does not cover
raises, never an invented result; the README, `AGENTS.md`, the guided path
and `tools.md` all tell people to trust that. `hpprime examples` is what
turns the emulator's answers into the `emulator` label, so what it stores is
the evidence itself. Each breaks its promise in a way that has been seen, and
this phase mends them before Phases 8.1 and 8 spend more keypresses.

Inserted on 2026-09-24 at the user's choice, from a review of the plan.

## Measured before planning, on 2026-09-24

### The interpreter

**An expression followed by a word it does not know ends there, and the rest
is read as further statements.** The parser's `semicolon()` accepts zero or
more `;`, so a statement never has to end with one. `RETURN 9 MOD 4 + 100;`
parses as `RETURN 9`, then a statement `MOD`, then a statement `4 + 100`.
The return happens first, so `hpprime run` answers 9, and the linter is
clean. As an assignment, `z := 9 MOD 4 + 100;`, the same split raises
"undefined variable: MOD", which is a refusal with the wrong reason.

**It accepts the one form of `MOD` the calculator refuses, and refuses the
one it accepts.** On the emulator `9 MOD 4` answers 1 and `MOD(9,4)` does
not compile ([MOD](../../../docs/commands/arithmetic/MOD.md)). The
interpreter has `MOD` as a function. `NTHROOT` is the same shape: `3 NTHROOT
8` answers 2 on the emulator and `NTHROOT(3,8)` is refused
([NTHROOT](../../../docs/commands/catalog/NTHROOT.md)). They are the two
operator words the documentation has measured.

**A builtin handed a type it does not handle dies with a Python traceback.**
`CONCAT({1,2,3},4)` gives `TypeError: 'float' object is not iterable`; the
list forms of `CEILING`, `FLOOR`, `FP`, `IP` and `ROUND` do the same. The
builtins are lambdas that do not check what they are given. `hpprime docs`
already treats these as "not covered", so the documentation tolerates what
the command line crashes on.

### The batch harness

**A probe can replace a stored row without a word.** `write_results()`
replaces any row with the same entry and call text. A probe typed by hand on
2026-09-15 reused `(Alpha)` and turned a refusal measured with the Function
app active into 0.01 measured with Inference active. Nothing warned, before
the keypresses or after.

**One cell the decoder cannot read loses the whole batch.**
`numbers.decode()` accepts 0 and 9 as the sign nibble and raises on anything
else, and `read_hpmat()` raises with it, so `collect()` writes nothing.
Measured on 2026-09-13: `valuation(X^2+X)` is negative infinity, exponent 499,
a mantissa of nines and sign nibble 2; `Dirac(0)` is positive infinity, the
same with sign nibble 6; `MAXREAL`, the same exponent and mantissa with an
ordinary sign, decoded as 9.99999999999E499. Two batches were lost this way,
and their rows recovered by hand afterwards.

**What an unreadable cell costs depends on which cell it is.** A row holds
whether the call answered, its type, the number when the type is 0, and the
answer's text as character codes, which is what an entry's Result shows. So
when only the number is unreadable, the text still says what the answer was.

**A row is dated the day the batch was prepared.** `run()` stamps the date
into the saved state before the emulator opens, and `collect()` uses it. A
batch prepared before midnight and run after it gives rows dated the day
before; the first geometry batch is the case.

## Decisions

The user chose 1 to 4 on 2026-09-24, each the recommended option; 5 to 7 were
approved with the context.

1. **`MOD` and `NTHROOT` match the calculator.** `9 MOD 4` and `3 NTHROOT
   8` answer as the emulator did; `MOD(9,4)` and `NTHROOT(3,8)` are refused
   with the calculator's reason. Two measured forms, and a deliberate
   exception to milestone 1's rule that the interpreter does not grow: it
   corrects a form it accepted wrongly. Chosen over both forms raising "not
   covered", which would have left `hpprime run` unable to check any program
   using `MOD`.
2. **When a stored row would change, in both places.** At collect time, a
   row whose stored answer differs is not replaced: the new answer is reported beside the old,
   and `hpprime examples --collect --replace` accepts it. At prepare time, a
   probe whose call text already has a row is refused before the emulator
   opens, since that is the shape of the 2026-09-15 mistake. Chosen over
   either place alone.
3. **An unreadable cell.** The two measured infinities are read: sign nibble
   2 or 6 with exponent 499 and twelve nines, and nothing wider. Any other
   unreadable cell costs its row only: kept from its text when only the
   number is unreadable, reported and left out of the file otherwise. Chosen
   over reading the infinities and still stopping on anything else.
4. **The date.** A row carries the modification date of `M9.hpmat`, which the
   emulator writes when it closes after the run. Stored rows keep their dates.
   Chosen over the date `--collect` runs, which differs when the answers are
   read another day.

Approved with the context:

5. **A statement ends at `;`, at the keyword that closes its block, or at the
   end of the file.** Anything else after an expression raises "not covered",
   naming the word. That is what turns `9 MOD 4 + 100` from a wrong answer
   into a refusal, whatever is decided for `MOD` itself, and it covers every
   operator word nobody has found yet.
6. **A builtin that fails on what it was given raises "not covered"**, naming
   the builtin and the types, instead of a traceback. Caught once, where
   builtins are called, not builtin by builtin.
7. **The entries that describe the old behaviour are corrected**: `MOD`'s
   paragraph on the interpreter, and the notes `hpprime docs` prints for the
   eight HP examples that crashed.

## Success criteria (from the roadmap)

1. `hpprime run` answers correctly, or raises with a message, on an operator
   word written between its operands, such as `MOD`, and on a builtin handed a
   type it does not handle, such as `CONCAT` given a number: never a partial
   answer and never a Python traceback. A test holds each case
2. `hpprime examples` refuses a probe whose call already holds a different
   stored answer, unless told to replace it, and says which rows it would
   change
3. An answer the decoder cannot read costs its own row, not the batch; the
   two infinities measured on 2026-09-13, sign nibbles 2 and 6, are read as
   infinities
4. A stored row carries the date its batch ran, not the date it was prepared

## Deferred

- The other operator words PPL may have. Decision 5 makes each one a refusal
  until somebody measures it.
- Whether `-9 MOD 4` answers 3 or -1 on the calculator: unmeasured, and the
  interpreter's Euclidean remainder is HP's help, not a measurement.
- Recovering the 25 geometry rows' true date: they stay as stored.
