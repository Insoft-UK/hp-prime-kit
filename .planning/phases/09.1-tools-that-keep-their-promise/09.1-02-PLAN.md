<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: The two infinities</name>
  <files>hpkit/numbers.py, tests/test_numbers.py, docs/topics/formats.md</files>
  <action>`decode()` reads sign nibble 2 with exponent 499 and twelve nines
  as negative infinity, and 6 with the same as positive infinity: the two
  measured on 2026-09-13. Any other unexpected nibble still raises, and so
  does 2 or 6 with any other exponent or mantissa. `read_hpmat()` takes
  `strict=False` to give None for a cell it cannot read, instead of raising
  for the whole matrix. The fact on the number format says what the two
  nibbles mean and how it is known. Tests: both infinities from their
  measured bytes; nibble 3, and nibble 2 with exponent 498, still refused; a
  matrix with one bad cell reads with None there when not strict.</action>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: An unreadable cell costs its row</name>
  <files>hpkit/examples.py, tests/test_examples_run.py</files>
  <action>`collect()` reads the matrix not strictly. A row whose number cell
  alone is unreadable keeps its answer from the text; any other unreadable
  cell makes the row *unreadable*, reported and not stored, while the rest
  of the batch is. Tests: a batch with one bad number cell and one bad text
  cell stores every row but the second.</action>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: A stored row is not replaced in silence</name>
  <files>hpkit/examples.py, tests/test_examples_run.py</files>
  <action>`collect()` compares each new row with the stored one by answer
  and type. A different one is kept out of the file and reported with the
  stored answer beside it, and the run exits 1; `--replace`, given to the
  run or to `--collect`, lets it through. `run()` refuses, before the
  emulator opens, a probe whose entry and call already have a row, unless
  `--replace`. Tests: a different answer is held and then replaced with
  replace; a same answer is written; a probe on a stored call is refused
  before anything launches.</action>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 4: The date the batch ran</name>
  <files>hpkit/examples.py, tests/test_examples_run.py, docs/tools.md</files>
  <action>A row's date is the day `M9.hpmat` was last written, which the
  emulator does when it closes after the run. The date is no longer kept in
  the saved state. `tools.md` says what `--replace` does and what a row's
  date is. Test: a matrix dated by hand gives rows with that date.</action>
  <verify><automated>python tests/run_all.py</automated></verify>
</task>
